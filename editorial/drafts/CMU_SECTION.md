# After Afriat: Pairwise Rationality and Coalitional Multi-Utility

> **Placement.** Insert this section in the revealed-preference chapter,
> immediately after Afriat's theorem and before prediction and welfare. The
> section assumes that the reader already knows utility maximization, GARP,
> and the finite-data statement of Afriat's theorem.

Afriat's theorem gives a sharp answer to a classical question: when can all of
a consumer's observations be explained by the maximization of one utility
function? Its answer is GARP. A failure of GARP therefore rejects that joint
explanation, but it need not mean that every comparison in the data is
inconsistent. A dataset may contain a revealed-preference cycle of length
three or more while every pair of observations remains coherent.

This distinction motivates a second, deliberately weaker question. When can
the data be explained by pairwise evaluations that never reverse a strict
comparison, even though the evaluations need not be generated by one
transitive ranking? The answer is WGARP. Coalitional multi-utility (CMU)
provides the corresponding optimizing model. Most importantly, CMU is an
extension of the benchmark model, not a competing starting point: ordinary
utility maximization is exactly its one-utility special case.

The presentation below follows the finite-data results of
\citet{aguiarhjertstrandserranoevren2026}. We first freeze the environment and
the four axioms, because WARP, WGARP, GARP, and SARP are not interchangeable.

## The finite budget environment

There are \(L\geq 2\) goods and finitely many observations

\[
O^T=\{(p^t,x^t)\}_{t\in\mathbb T},
\qquad \mathbb T=\{1,\ldots,T\}.
\]

The consumption set is \(X=\mathbb R_+^L\). For every \(t\), assume
\(x^t\in X\setminus\{0\}\), \(p^t\in\mathbb R_{++}^L\), and

\[
w^t=p^t\cdot x^t.
\]

Thus observed expenditure exhausts observed wealth. For any
\((p,w)\in\mathbb R_{++}^L\times\mathbb R_{++}\), write

\[
B(p,w)=\{x\in X:p\cdot x\leq w\}.
\]

For vectors \(x,z\in X\), \(x>z\) means \(x\geq z\) component by component
and \(x\neq z\); \(x\gg z\) means strict inequality in every component.

The direct revealed-preference relations are

\[
x^t\succeq^{R,D}x^s
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
p^t\cdot x^t\geq p^t\cdot x^s,
\]

and

\[
x^t\succ^{R,D}x^s
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
p^t\cdot x^t>p^t\cdot x^s.
\]

Let \(\succeq^R\) denote the transitive closure of
\(\succeq^{R,D}\). The following table records exactly which cycles each axiom
excludes.

| Axiom | Forbidden pattern |
|---|---|
| WGARP | \(x^t\succeq^{R,D}x^s\) and \(x^s\succ^{R,D}x^t\) |
| WARP | \(x^t\neq x^s\), \(x^t\succeq^{R,D}x^s\), and \(x^s\succeq^{R,D}x^t\) |
| GARP | \(x^t\succeq^R x^s\) and \(x^s\succ^{R,D}x^t\) |
| SARP | \(x^t\neq x^s\), \(x^t\succeq^R x^s\), and \(x^s\succeq^{R,D}x^t\) |

WGARP is the weak/strict condition suited to a demand correspondence. WARP is
the stricter binary condition associated with single-valued demand: distinct
chosen bundles cannot be mutually affordable. GARP and SARP apply their
respective prohibition after taking the transitive closure. In particular,
one must not substitute WARP for WGARP in the representation theorem below.

## Preference functions and maximizing demand

A **preference function** is a map

\[
r:X\times X\longrightarrow\mathbb R.
\]

It induces a binary relation by

\[
x\succeq_r y
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
r(x,y)\geq 0.
\]

The strict part is defined in the usual relational way:

\[
x\succ_r y
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
x\succeq_r y\ \text{and not }y\succeq_r x.
\]

Thus \(x\succ_r y\) should not be silently redefined as \(r(x,y)>0\).
Those two statements agree under additional conditions used below, but they
are not identical by definition.

We use three properties of \(r\).

1. It is **strictly increasing** if, for all \(x,y,z\in X\),
   \(x>z\) implies \(r(x,y)>r(z,y)\).
2. It is **asymmetric** if

   \[
   r(x,y)\geq 0\Longrightarrow r(y,x)\leq 0,
   \qquad
   r(x,y)>0\Longrightarrow r(y,x)<0.
   \]
3. It is **skew-symmetric** if
   \(r(x,y)=-r(y,x)\) for all \(x,y\in X\).

Skew-symmetry implies asymmetry, but the converse need not hold. These are
properties of the numerical comparison rule, not an assumption that the
induced relation is transitive.

The demand correspondence generated by \(r\) is the set of alternatives that
weakly defeat every feasible alternative:

\[
D_r(p,w)
=
\{x\in B(p,w):r(x,y)\geq 0\text{ for every }y\in B(p,w)\}.
\tag{CMU-D}
\]

The function \(r\) **rationalizes** \(O^T\) if
\(x^t\in D_r(p^t,w^t)\) for every \(t\in\mathbb T\). This maximal-element
notion is stronger than merely asking that the chosen bundle be undominated.
For an incomplete or intransitive relation, \(D_r(p,w)\) can be empty even
when the budget is compact.

## Coherent coalitional multi-utility

Let \(C(X)\) be the space of continuous real-valued functions on \(X\),
equipped with the topology of uniform convergence on compact subsets. To make
the compactness requirement precise, set

\[
K_n=\{x\in X:\|x\|\leq n\},\qquad
d_n(u,v)=\sup_{x\in K_n}|u(x)-v(x)|,
\]

and use the compatible metric

\[
d(u,v)=
\sum_{n\in\mathbb N}2^{-n}\frac{d_n(u,v)}{1+d_n(u,v)}.
\]

A **coalition structure** is a nonempty collection \(\Omega\) of nonempty sets
\(U\subseteq C(X)\). Each \(U\in\Omega\) is called a coalition. Let \(d_H\)
denote the Hausdorff metric induced by \(d\). The coalition structure is
**compact** when every \(U\in\Omega\) is compact under \(d\) and \(\Omega\)
is compact under \(d_H\). For a compact coalition structure, define

\[
r_\Omega(x,y)
=
\max_{U\in\Omega}\min_{u\in U}\bigl(u(x)-u(y)\bigr).
\tag{CMU}
\]

This is the CMU preference function. A comparison \(x\succeq_\Omega y\)
holds exactly when at least one coalition unanimously assigns \(x\) at least
as much utility as \(y\). Compactness makes the displayed maximum and minimum
well defined and makes \(r_\Omega\) continuous. If every utility in every
coalition is strictly increasing, then \(r_\Omega\) is strictly increasing in
its first argument and strictly decreasing in its second.

A coalition structure is **coherent** if

\[
U\cap V\neq\varnothing
\qquad\text{for every }U,V\in\Omega.
\tag{Coherence}
\]

This is pairwise intersection. It does not assert that all coalitions share a
single utility function. Coherence is the feature that prevents binary
reversals: every coherent CMU function is asymmetric.

### Classical utility maximization is nested exactly

**Proposition (singleton nesting).** Let \(u\in C(X)\) and set
\(\Omega_u=\{\{u\}\}\). Then \(\Omega_u\) is a compact coherent coalition
structure,

\[
r_{\Omega_u}(x,y)=u(x)-u(y),
\]

and, for every \((p,w)\in\mathbb R_{++}^L\times\mathbb R_{++}\),

\[
D_{r_{\Omega_u}}(p,w)
=
\underset{x\in B(p,w)}{\arg\max}\ u(x).
\tag{Nesting}
\]

**Proof.** Because \(\Omega_u\) contains only the singleton \(\{u\}\), both
operators in (CMU) collapse and
\(r_{\Omega_u}(x,y)=u(x)-u(y)\). Consequently,

\[
\begin{aligned}
x\in D_{r_{\Omega_u}}(p,w)
&\Longleftrightarrow
u(x)-u(y)\geq 0\quad\text{for every }y\in B(p,w)\\
&\Longleftrightarrow
x\in\arg\max_{z\in B(p,w)}u(z).
\end{aligned}
\]

The singleton coalition is compact and intersects itself, so it is coherent.
\(\square\)

Continuity is the only property of \(u\) needed to place this singleton in the
CMU environment. Strict monotonicity, concavity, or strict concavity is
inherited only when \(u\) itself has that property. The nesting claim should
therefore not be stated with stronger assumptions than its proof uses.

The logical relation is:

```text
one continuous utility u
    |
    | Omega = {{u}} exactly
    v
singleton coherent CMU -----> general coherent CMU
    |                              |
    | + local nonsatiation         | + strict increase and coherence
    |   on finite budget data      |   on finite budget data
    v                              v
   GARP -------------------------> WGARP
```

The horizontal arrows are relaxations, not equivalences. A general coherent
CMU may be incomplete or intransitive, and WGARP does not imply GARP when
there are at least three goods.

## The pairwise Afriat theorem

**Theorem* (WGARP and coherent CMU, reader version).** In the finite budget
environment stated above, the following statements are equivalent.

1. The dataset is rationalized by an asymmetric, strictly increasing, and
   continuous preference function \(r:X\times X\to\mathbb R\).
2. The dataset is rationalized by a coherent, strictly increasing, and
   continuous CMU function.
3. The dataset is rationalized by a skew-symmetric, coherent, and continuous
   CMU function \(r_\Omega\) for which every coalition consists of strictly
   increasing, concave utility functions.
4. The dataset satisfies WGARP.

The asterisk signals that the main text gives the economically operative
statement while the technical appendix records the complete theorem and its
proof. The result is an exact finite-data characterization; it is not merely
a necessary condition.

Here is the proof architecture. A strictly increasing, asymmetric preference
function cannot rationalize a strict two-observation reversal, so it implies
WGARP. Conversely, if the full dataset satisfies WGARP, then every
two-observation subdataset satisfies GARP. Afriat's theorem supplies a
continuous, strictly increasing, concave utility \(u_{st}=u_{ts}\) for each
pair \(\{s,t\}\). These pairwise utilities can be assembled into a symmetric
utility matrix and then into a compact coherent coalition structure. The
resulting CMU rationalizes all observations simultaneously. The technical
appendix supplies the construction and verifies compactness, coherence, and
skew-symmetry.

Two interpretations are worth separating. The theorem says that WGARP is the
observable content of this class of coherent pairwise models. It does not say
that a WGARP dataset reveals nontransitivity: any GARP dataset also passes
WGARP and has both an ordinary utility rationalization and a CMU
rationalization.

## WARP requires a separate strict theorem

WGARP permits two distinct chosen bundles to be mutually affordable when both
budget comparisons bind. WARP excludes that case. The extra empirical
strictness corresponds to an extra local curvature property of the preference
function.

A preference function \(r\) is **strictly star-concave** if, for every
\(x\neq y\) and every \(\alpha\in(0,1)\),

\[
r\bigl(\alpha x+(1-\alpha)y,x\bigr)
>
\alpha r(x,x)+(1-\alpha)r(y,x).
\tag{Star}
\]

This condition asks for strict concavity of the map \(r(\cdot,x)\) only along
segments ending at its second argument \(x\). It is not global concavity of
\(r\).

**Theorem* (WARP and strict coherent CMU, reader version).** In the same
finite budget environment, the following statements are equivalent.

1. The dataset is rationalized by an asymmetric, strictly star-concave, and
   continuous preference function.
2. The dataset is rationalized by a coherent, strictly star-concave, and
   continuous CMU function.
3. The dataset is rationalized by a skew-symmetric, coherent, and continuous
   CMU function \(r_\Omega\) for which every coalition consists of strictly
   increasing, strictly concave utility functions.
4. The dataset satisfies WARP.

This theorem must not be merged with the WGARP theorem. In particular, WARP
alone is not the finite-data condition for maximization of one ordinary
utility function. GARP is the classical weak rationalizability condition, and
SARP is the corresponding acyclic condition used for strict, single-valued
utility rationalization. The WARP theorem instead characterizes a stricter
class of possibly intransitive CMU comparisons. Asymmetry and strict
star-concavity imply that \(D_r(p,w)\) contains at most one bundle whenever it
is nonempty; the theorem still does not guarantee nonemptiness on every
counterfactual budget.

## A three-good dataset that separates WGARP from GARP

Let

\[
\begin{array}{lll}
p^1=(4,1,5), & p^2=(5,4,1), & p^3=(1,5,4),\\
x^1=(4,1,1), & x^2=(1,4,1), & x^3=(1,1,4).
\end{array}
\]

Rows in the following matrix index prices \(p^t\), and columns index bundles
\(x^s\):

\[
\bigl[p^t\cdot x^s\bigr]_{t,s=1}^3
=
\begin{pmatrix}
22&13&25\\
25&22&13\\
13&25&22
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

At the first budget, \(x^2\) costs \(13<22\), so
\(x^1\succ^{R,D}x^2\). Similarly,

\[
x^1\succ^{R,D}x^2
\succ^{R,D}x^3
\succ^{R,D}x^1.
\]

The strict three-cycle violates GARP. It does not generate a binary reversal:
for each displayed comparison, the reverse-budget cost is \(25>22\), so the
first chosen bundle was not affordable when the second was chosen. The data
therefore satisfy WGARP. Since the three chosen bundles are distinct and no
pair is mutually affordable, this particular dataset also satisfies WARP.

The example isolates what CMU adds. No single transitive utility ranking can
rationalize the three observations, but their pairwise budget evidence is
consistent. The distinction requires at least three goods in this linear
budget setting: with two goods, WGARP and GARP coincide.

## What survives: the compensated law of demand

Weakening transitivity does not discard every implication of the benchmark
model.

**Proposition (compensated law of demand).** Let \(r\) be asymmetric and
strictly increasing. Suppose \(x\in D_r(p,w)\). For any new price vector
\(p'\in\mathbb R_{++}^L\), compensate wealth so that the original bundle is
just affordable, \(w'=p'\cdot x\). Then every
\(x'\in D_r(p',w')\) satisfies

\[
(p'-p)\cdot(x'-x)\leq 0.
\tag{CLD}
\]

**Proof.** Strict increase and asymmetry imply budget exhaustion at every
nonempty demand: if an optimum were strictly inside its budget, a slightly
larger feasible bundle would strictly defeat it. Hence
\(p'\cdot x'=w'=p'\cdot x\), so

\[
p'\cdot(x'-x)=0.
\]

The two choices generated by \(r\) obey WGARP. At the new budget, \(x\) is
affordable at equality. WGARP therefore rules out
\(p\cdot x>p\cdot x'\), and thus
\(p\cdot(x'-x)\geq0\). Subtracting gives

\[
(p'-p)\cdot(x'-x)
=-p\cdot(x'-x)\leq0.
\]

\(\square\)

The proposition is conditional on \(D_r(p',w')\) being nonempty. It is a
finite-change inequality, not Slutsky symmetry and not an integrability
theorem.

## Box: what survives, and what does not

**Retained under the stated hypotheses**

- **Exact nesting.** A singleton coalition reproduces the ordinary UMP and
  its demand correspondence exactly.
- **Pairwise revealed-preference discipline.** Coherence supplies asymmetry;
  strict increase plus asymmetry implies WGARP.
- **Walras' law at chosen bundles.** If \(r\) is asymmetric and strictly
  increasing, every element of a nonempty \(D_r(p,w)\) exhausts the budget.
- **Homogeneity of degree zero.** Because
  \(B(\alpha p,\alpha w)=B(p,w)\) for \(\alpha>0\),
  \(D_r(\alpha p,\alpha w)=D_r(p,w)\), including the possibility that both
  sets are empty.
- **Compensated law of demand.** The inequality (CLD) holds whenever the two
  relevant demand sets contain the stated choices.

**Not implied by WGARP or coherence alone**

- one globally valid scalar utility index, transitivity, or GARP;
- completeness of every coherent CMU relation, although the finite-data
  representation in the theorem can be chosen skew-symmetric and hence
  complete;
- convex preferences: concavity of the component utilities yields a useful
  star-shaped property, not convexity of the induced CMU relation;
- existence of a maximizing bundle on every unobserved budget;
- Roy's identity, Shephard's lemma, the classical UMP-EMP duality, Slutsky
  symmetry, or smooth integrability without additional hypotheses;
- the usual scalar-utility welfare numbers and support-set bounds without a
  separate CMU welfare analysis.

These failures are boundaries of the theorem, not defects to conceal. They
tell the reader exactly which conclusions came from transitivity in the
benchmark theory.

## Interpretation and cautions

1. **Coherence is essential.** An arbitrary CMU need not satisfy WGARP. For
   example, placing each candidate utility in its own singleton coalition is
   generally not coherent and can generate binary reversals.
2. **Pairwise overlap is not a common self.** The condition
   \(U\cap V\neq\varnothing\) for every pair does not imply
   \(\bigcap_{U\in\Omega}U\neq\varnothing\). Treating it as global
   intersection would collapse the model toward ordinary utility and erase
   the cycles it is designed to allow.
3. **Concave components do not make the CMU relation convex.** Max-min
   aggregation does not preserve the global convexity needed for the standard
   convex-preference results.
4. **Observed rationalization is not global existence.** The theorem ensures
   that each observed choice is a maximal element of its observed budget. It
   does not ensure that \(D_r(p,w)\) is nonempty at every counterfactual
   \((p,w)\).
5. **The model is not identified by passing WGARP.** A GARP dataset passes
   WGARP too. The data may therefore admit both transitive and nontransitive
   rationalizations.
6. **The maximum and minimum rely on compactness.** Without the stated
   compactness of coalitions and their collection, write supremum and infimum
   and recheck attainment and continuity.
7. **CMU here is deterministic.** It is not a random-utility, stochastic
   choice, limited-attention, or rational-inattention model. Those models
   answer different questions and belong later in the consumer block.

material belongs in a technical appendix so that no mathematical content is

### Full WGARP theorem record

Append the two computational statements to the four equivalent statements in
the reader theorem.

5. There exist real numbers \(R^{s,t}\) and positive numbers
   \(\lambda^s_{s,t},\lambda^t_{s,t}\) for every \(s,t\in\mathbb T\), with

   \[
   R^{s,t}=-R^{t,s},
   \qquad
   \lambda^s_{s,t}=\lambda^s_{t,s},
   \]

   such that

   \[
   R^{s,t}\geq
   \lambda^s_{s,t}p^s\cdot(x^s-x^t)
   \qquad\text{for every }s,t\in\mathbb T.
   \tag{Pairwise Afriat}
   \]
6. There exist antisymmetric numbers
   \(V^{s,t}=-V^{t,s}\) such that, for every \(s,t\in\mathbb T\),

   \[
   \begin{cases}
   p^s\cdot(x^s-x^t)\geq0
     \Longrightarrow V^{s,t}\geq0,\\
   p^s\cdot(x^s-x^t)>0
     \Longrightarrow V^{s,t}>0.
   \end{cases}
   \tag{Pairwise Varian}
   \]

The appendix should prove all six statements equivalent, not present the two
inequality systems as optional remarks. It should also state the topology on
\(C(X)\), prove continuity of the CMU, prove that coherence implies asymmetry,
and give the symmetric utility-matrix construction used in the difficult
direction WGARP \(\Rightarrow\) CMU.

### Full WARP theorem record

The appendix should reproduce the four-way WARP equivalence separately and
prove the strict star-concavity lemma. The proof uses strictly concave
pairwise utility rationalizations; it must not be presented as a corollary of
the WGARP theorem obtained merely by changing the name of the axiom.
