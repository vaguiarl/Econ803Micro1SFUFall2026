# Revealed Preference and Recoverability

## R001 | Proof | Quasilinear Afriat inequalities and welfare bounds

Let $\mathcal O=\{(p^t,x^t)\}_{t=1}^T$, where $p^t\gg 0$ and $x^t\in\mathbb R_+^L$.

**Part I — by hand.** Prove that the observations can satisfy $x^t\in\arg\max_{x\in\mathbb R_+^L}\{u(x)-p^t\cdot x\}$ only if there are numbers $v^1,\ldots,v^T$ such that $v^t-v^s\ge p^t\cdot(x^t-x^s)$ for every $s,t$. Prove the converse by studying $u(x)=\min_t\{v^t+p^t\cdot(x-x^t)\}$. Establish continuity, concavity, strict monotonicity, and rationalization rather than merely citing Afriat's theorem.

For $V(p)=\max_{x\in\mathbb R_+^L}\{u(x)-p\cdot x\}$, suppose optimizers $x(p)$ and $x(q)$ exist. Derive upper and lower bounds on $V(p)-V(q)$ using the two observed demands. Extend the argument to a finite path of price vectors and state precisely when a componentwise price increase gives a strict welfare loss.

**Part II — verified scale-up.** Express the inequalities as a difference-constraints graph. With Codex and Python, implement a feasibility test for arbitrary $T$ and $L$, return one normalized vector $v$, and report a violating positive cycle when the system is infeasible. Unit-test the code on a small rationalizable data set and on a perturbed data set. Verify every returned inequality directly. As an optional Lean task, formalize the telescoping path bound conditional on the finite system of inequalities.

# Abstract Choice and Order Foundations

## R002 | Proof | Sequential satisficing with a default rule

Let $X$ be finite, let $\triangleright$ be a strict linear (total) search order, and let $S=\{x:u(x)\ge\bar u\}$. From a nonempty menu $A$, inspect alternatives in the order $\triangleright$ and choose the first member of $A\cap S$. If $A\cap S$ is empty, choose the $\triangleright$-first member of $A$.

**Part I — by hand.** Construct a single strict priority order $\succ^*$ whose maximal element agrees with this procedure on every nonempty menu. Use that representation to prove WARP and SARP. Explain exactly why the fallback clause is necessary for a full-domain choice function. Give a three-alternative example and list the choice from every nonempty menu.

**Part II — verified scale-up.** Encode finite menus, the search order, the threshold set, and the fallback choice. Ask Codex to generate either a Lean proof or an exhaustive checker establishing equality between sequential choice and $\succ^*$-maximization. Scale from three alternatives to all menus of a user-chosen finite set, and include a deliberately broken implementation without the fallback to exhibit its failure of full-domain nonemptiness.

## R003 | Applied | Choice overload and attraction through menu-dependent attention

An outside option $o$ is always considered. In a menu with $n$ active products, each active product is independently considered with baseline probability $q_n=1/n^2$; the consumer chooses the highest-$u$ considered item, or $o$ if none is considered. Utilities are strict. When a target $b$ and its dominated decoy $b^-$ are both offered, the consideration probability of $b$ rises by $\delta=1/3$, while all other baseline probabilities remain unchanged.

**Part I — by hand.** Compare $A_2=\{a,c,o\}$ with $A_3=\{a,c,d,o\}$ and calculate the probability of choosing $o$. Next let $u(b)>u(c)>u(b^-)>u(o)$ and compare $A=\{b,c,o\}$ with $B=\{b,b^-,c,o\}$. Calculate the probability of choosing $b$ in both menus. Prove that the resulting attraction inequality cannot be generated by any menu-independent random-utility model.

**Part II — verified scale-up.** Build an exact enumerator over consideration sets and a Monte Carlo simulation. Confirm the hand calculations, then vary $n$, $\delta$, and the ranking. Ask Codex to produce automated checks for probability adding-up, the parameter domain, regularity violations, and convergence of the simulation to the exact probabilities. Report which changes generate overload, attraction, both, or neither.

## R004 | Proof | Maximal choice under incompleteness and a GARP failure

Let $X=\{a,b,c\}$. Define an incomplete preorder $R$ by reflexivity together with $bPa$; alternative $c$ is incomparable with both $a$ and $b$. From each nonempty menu $A$, let $C(A)$ contain all $R$-maximal elements.

**Part I — by hand.** Compute $C(A)$ for every nonempty menu. Define direct revealed preference by $xR^Dy$ when some menu chooses $x$ while making $y$ feasible, and define $xP^Dy$ when that menu rejects $y$. Show that the transitive closure $(R^D)^*$, together with $P^D$, violates the standard GARP test; identify an explicit witness. Explain why this does not contradict maximality under an incomplete preorder.

**Part II — verified scale-up.** Represent finite preorders and maximal-choice correspondences in Lean or Python. Verify reflexivity and transitivity of the primitive relation, compute all maximal sets, and mechanically exhibit the revealed-preference path. Then enumerate all three-alternative preorders and classify which induced maximal-choice data satisfy GARP. Distinguish a failure of the data test from a failure of the primitive preorder axioms.

# Production and Competitive Markets

## R005 | Proof | Aggregating heterogeneous Cobb–Douglas firms

Firm $j$ has technology $q_j=A_j\ell_j^{a_j}k_j^{b_j}$ with $A_j>0$, $a_j,b_j>0$, and $a_j+b_j<1$. Its net-output set is $Y_j=\{(-\ell,-k,q):q\le A_j\ell^{a_j}k^{b_j},\ \ell,k\ge0\}$. Output price is $p>0$ and input prices are $w_\ell,w_k>0$.

**Part I — by hand.** Derive the unique profit-maximizing output, both input demands, and profit. State where decreasing returns are used. Prove that the aggregate technology is the Minkowski sum $Y=Y_1+\cdots+Y_J$ and that its profit function is the sum of the firm profit functions. State the corresponding Hotelling result both at differentiable prices and in subdifferential form, using derivatives $(q_j,-\ell_j,-k_j)$ with respect to $(p,w_\ell,w_k)$.

**Part II — verified scale-up.** Generate a heterogeneous population of firms and compute aggregate supply and factor demands. Verify numerically that direct maximization over firm-level allocations agrees with the sum of closed-form firm solutions. Use automatic or symbolic differentiation to check Hotelling's lemma, and test how the aggregate elasticity changes with the distribution of returns-to-scale parameters.

## R006 | Applied | Selection versus capacity destruction in partial equilibrium

There is a mass $\eta>0$ of consumers with $u(x,m;\alpha)=2\sqrt{\alpha x}+m$ and $\alpha\sim U[0,1]$. The numeraire is unrestricted, or wealth is sufficient for the interior solution. There is initially a unit mass of firms with $C(q;e)=q^2/(2e)$, where productivity has the uniform law on $e\in(0,1)$; endpoint conventions are immaterial for aggregation.

**Part I — by hand.** Derive individual and aggregate demand, firm supply and profit, and the competitive equilibrium price. Verify Hotelling's lemma. Compare two shocks: (i) the unit mass of firms is replaced by a unit mass with $e\sim U[1/2,1]$; (ii) firms with $e<1/2$ disappear without replacement. Solve for both new prices and explain why selection and capacity destruction have opposite price effects. At the baseline equilibrium, compute the cross-sectional variance of consumption and mean indirect utility when numeraire wealth is constant.

**Part II — verified scale-up.** Replace the uniform type distributions by beta distributions and allow an arbitrary survival cutoff. Derive the market-clearing equation, solve it numerically over a parameter grid, and separate the effect of changing the conditional productivity distribution from changing producer mass. Include monotonicity checks and compare numerical integration with the uniform closed form.

# Equilibrium, Trade, and Computation

## R007 | Applied | CES structural gravity and multilateral resistance

Destination $j$ has CES utility $U_j=[\sum_i a_i^{1/\sigma}c_{ij}^{(\sigma-1)/\sigma}]^{\sigma/(\sigma-1)}$ with $\sigma>1$ and $a_i,p_i,\tau_{ij},y_j>0$. Delivered prices are $p_{ij}=p_i\tau_{ij}$ and bilateral expenditure is $x_{ij}:=p_i\tau_{ij}c_{ij}$. Total income is $Y=\sum_jy_j$, and balanced expenditure and income impose market clearing $y_i=\sum_jx_{ij}$.

**Part I — by hand.** Derive the CES price index, bilateral quantities, and bilateral expenditure. With $\theta_j=y_j/Y$, define an outward multilateral-resistance index and derive the structural-gravity equation together with the inward-index system. Take logs and identify the exporter effect, importer effect, and trade-cost coefficient. Explain the reciprocal scale freedom and state a normalization; do not claim that symmetric trade costs alone pin down every unnormalized inward and outward index as equal.

**Part II — verified scale-up.** For a synthetic economy with at least ten countries, solve the normalized multilateral-resistance system in log variables. Verify market clearing, bilateral adding-up, positivity, and invariance to the discarded scale normalization. Compare fixed-point iteration with a root solver and report convergence diagnostics. Recover the trade-cost coefficient from a regression with exporter and importer fixed effects.

## R008 | Proof | Why a Banach argument fails even when existence and uniqueness hold

On $K=[1/2,2]^2$, consider $g:K\to\mathbb R^2$ with identical components $g_j(P)=[(P_1^{-1/2}+P_2^{-1/2})/2]^2$.

**Part I — by hand.** Prove that $g$ is a continuous self-map of $K$ and use the appropriate fixed-point theorem to establish existence. Compute $Dg(1,1)$, its eigenvalues, and its Euclidean operator norm. Explain why no contraction proof in any induced norm can follow from the stated map. Nevertheless, prove directly that the positive fixed point is unique. Exhibit a two-cycle of the un-damped Picard iteration.

**Part II — verified scale-up.** Use interval arithmetic or Lean-supported inequalities to certify invariance of $K$ and the derivative calculation. Compare plain iteration, the damped rule $P^{n+1}=(1-\lambda)P^n+\lambda g(P^n)$ for $\lambda=1/2$, and a root solver from multiple starting values. Ask Codex to create tests that distinguish existence, uniqueness, local stability, and global contraction; the report must not infer one property from another.

## R009 | Proof | A regular two-good economy with three equilibria

Normalize prices to $(q,1)$. Consumer 1 chooses $a\ge0,b>0$, has $u_1(a,b)=a-\tfrac18b^{-8}$, and owns $(2,r)$. Consumer 2 chooses $c>0,d\ge0$, has $u_2(c,d)=-\tfrac18c^{-8}+d$, and owns $(r,2)$, where $r=2^{8/9}-2^{1/9}$.

**Part I — by hand.** Derive both consumers' interior demands and prove that $a(q),d(q)>0$ for every $q>0$, so boundary demand cannot create an omitted equilibrium. Derive the good-2 excess-demand function. Show that $q\in\{1/2,1,2\}$ are equilibria and that there are no other positive normalized equilibria. Verify that each equilibrium is regular and locally isolated. Compute $z_2'(1)$ and compare it with the gross-substitutes requirement $z_2'(1)\ge0$. Explain why regularity does not imply uniqueness.

**Part II — verified scale-up.** Put $t=q^{1/9}$ and use a computer algebra system to certify the positive-root count without relying only on a plot. Check all equilibrium residuals and derivatives at high precision. Choose one scalar endowment perturbation, initialize continuation at each baseline equilibrium, and trace each local branch only until regularity fails or the declared small parameter interval ends. Connect the local numerical result to the implicit-function theorem. Optionally formalize the exact three roots and their nonzero derivatives in Lean.

## R010 | Applied | A complete-markets Radner equilibrium by hand

There are two consumers, two states $A,B$, and two goods in each state. Both consumers have equal state probabilities and state utility $\log c_1+\log c_2$. Each owns $\omega>0$ units of good 2 in each state. Consumer 1's good-1 endowment is $(\omega/2,3\omega/2)$ across states, and consumer 2's is reversed. Good 1 is the state numeraire and $r_s$ is the state-$s$ price of good 2. Arrow security $s$ pays one unit of good 1 in state $s$. Consumer $i$ holds $\theta_{is}$, securities are in zero net supply, and the date-0 asset budget is $Q_A\theta_{iA}+Q_B\theta_{iB}=0$.

**Part I — by hand.** Normalize the state-$B$ security price to $Q_B=1$ and write $q=Q_A$. Derive individual security demands as functions of $q,r_A,r_B$. Show that commodity clearing under equal spot prices requires $r_A=r_B=1$ and that the equilibrium security-price ratio is one. Calculate portfolios and state consumption, and verify both commodity and security clearing. Explain the role of strict concavity and price normalization in the uniqueness statement.

**Part II — verified scale-up.** Generalize the computation to $S$ states and a full set of Arrow securities. Build the sequential budget matrix, solve the equilibrium numerically, and verify state-by-state feasibility and zero net security supply. Ask Codex or Lean to check the linear identity mapping sequential budgets into the Arrow–Debreu present-value budget, clearly separating the algebraic equivalence from equilibrium existence.
