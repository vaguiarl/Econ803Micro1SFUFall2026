# Basics of Consumer Theory

## 1.1 | Core | Budget scaling and Cobb-Douglas demand

Let $X=\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}$ and $B(p,w)=\{x\in X:p\cdot x\leq w\}$, where $p\gg0$ and $w>0$.

(a) Prove that $B(\lambda p,\lambda w)=B(p,w)$ for every $\lambda>0$. Explain why every demand correspondence generated by preference maximization is homogeneous of degree zero in $(p,w)$.

(b) Suppose preferences are locally nonsatiated. Prove that every chosen bundle satisfies Walras' law, $p\cdot x=w$.

(c) For $u(x)=x_{1}^{\alpha}x_{2}^{1-\alpha}$ with $0<\alpha<1$, derive Marshallian demand and verify both properties directly.

## 1.2 | Core | A two-observation WARP test

A consumer chooses $x^{1}=(2,2)$ at $(p^{1},w^{1})=((2,1),6)$ and $x^{2}=(1,5/2)$ at $(p^{2},w^{2})=((1,2),6)$.

(a) Verify that each observed bundle exhausts its budget.

(b) Determine whether each bundle was affordable at the other observation.

(c) Does the dataset satisfy WARP for single-valued demand? Identify the two direct revealed-preference comparisons that determine your answer.

## 1.3 | Proof | Differential restrictions on demand

Let $x:\mathbb{R}_{++}^{L}\times\mathbb{R}_{++}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}^{L}$ be continuously differentiable and satisfy Walras' law.

(a) Differentiate Walras' law with respect to wealth and prices.

(b) Define the Slutsky matrix by $S(p,w)=D_{p}x(p,w)+D_{w}x(p,w)x(p,w)^{\top}$. Prove that $p^{\top}S(p,w)=0$.

(c) Explain which additional restrictions on $S$ follow from utility maximization and which of them do not follow from Walras' law alone.

# Preference and Choice

## 2.1 | Core | Incomplete preferences and multiple utilities

Let $X=\{a,b,c\}$ and let $R$ contain all reflexive pairs and the comparison $aRb$, but no other comparison between distinct alternatives.

(a) Show that $R$ is a preorder but is incomplete.

(b) Prove that no real-valued utility function can represent $R$ in the sense $xRy$ if and only if $u(x)\geq u(y)$.

(c) Construct two utility functions $u_{1},u_{2}$ such that $xRy$ if and only if $u_{k}(x)\geq u_{k}(y)$ for both $k=1,2$.

## 2.2 | Core | Choice cycles on finite menus

Let $X=\{a,b,c\}$ and suppose a single-valued choice function satisfies $c(\{a,b\})=a$, $c(\{b,c\})=b$, and $c(\{a,c\})=c$. Say that a preference relation $R$ rationalizes these data when the observed alternative is the unique $R$-greatest element of each observed menu.

(a) Write the direct revealed-preference comparisons generated by these choices.

(b) Show that no transitive preference relation can rationalize the data.

(c) State the smallest change to one observation that restores acyclicity, and exhibit a complete and transitive rationalization of the revised data.

## 2.3 | Proof | Maximization and set-valued WARP

Let $R$ be complete and transitive on a finite set $X$. For each nonempty menu $A\subseteq X$, let $c(A)$ be the set of $R$-greatest elements of $A$.

(a) Prove that $c(A)$ is nonempty.

(b) Suppose $x\in c(A)$, $y\in c(B)$, and $x,y\in A\cap B$. Prove that $xIy$, where $I$ is the symmetric part of $R$.

(c) Explain why this conclusion is the appropriate set-valued version of WARP.

# Behavioral Economics: Sparse-Max and Rational Inattention

## 4.2 | Core | CES demand under perceived prices

## 4.3 | Applied | A binary rational-inattention problem

There are two equally likely states and two actions. The decision maker receives payoff one from the correct action and zero from the incorrect action. A symmetric strategy is correct with probability $q\in[1/2,1]$ and pays Shannon-information cost $\kappa[\log2-H(q)]$, where $H(q)=-q\log q-(1-q)\log(1-q)$ and $\kappa>0$.

(a) Write expected net payoff as a function of $q$.

(b) Derive the interior optimum and show that $q^{*}=\exp(1/\kappa)/(1+\exp(1/\kappa))$.

(c) Describe the limits as $\kappa\downarrow0$ and $\kappa\uparrow\infty$.

## 5.3 | Proof | The Arrow-Pratt approximation

(b) State the regularity and small-risk qualifications needed for this approximation.

## 5.4 | Applied | CARA-normal portfolio demand
